# Swiss Nuclear Decommissioning Timeline

The fleet is aging, its retirement schedule is a direct input to the [[Winter Import Gap]],
and its legal future is still unresolved.

- **The fleet today:** 4 reactors remain after Mühleberg's 2019 shutdown — Beznau I & II
  (~6 TWh combined, one of the world's oldest operating reactors, scheduled retirement
  2029–2031 under a 60-year lifespan), Gösgen (~8.5 TWh), Leibstadt (~9.5 TWh, last unit
  connected 1984). See [[Nuclear Power]].
- **Baseline trajectory:** in the workshop dashboard's "Baseline (60-yr lifespan)" scenario,
  nuclear output steps down from ~23 TWh (pre-2029) to ~17 TWh by the early 2030s as Beznau
  retires.
- **Legal status in flux:** the 2017 popular vote banned new nuclear plants; the
  [[Swiss Nuclear Ban Referendum]] (2024–2026) may reverse that. But even if it does, no new
  plant can complete design, licensing, and construction within the 2026–2036 window
  (15–20+ years realistically, per international comparators like Flamanville and Hinkley
  Point C).
- **Consequence:** any recommendation must be robust to either referendum outcome, and treats
  new nuclear as a 2040s-and-beyond strategic option, not a 2030s supply source — see
  [[Recommended Portfolio]].
