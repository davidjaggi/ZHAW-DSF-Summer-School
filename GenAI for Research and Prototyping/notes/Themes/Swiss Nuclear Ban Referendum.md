# Swiss Nuclear Ban Referendum

Whether Switzerland can build new nuclear plants again is, as of this writing, genuinely
undecided — and the recommendation in this case study is designed to be robust to either
outcome.

- **Background:** the 2017 popular vote (Energy Strategy 2050) banned new nuclear plants.
- **The challenge:** the "Stop the Blackout" popular initiative (launched March 2024 by
  business/conservative groups) seeks a constitutional guarantee of secure, climate-neutral
  electricity supply, implicitly reopening the door to nuclear.
- **Counter-proposal:** in 2025 the Federal Council proposed amending the Nuclear Energy Act
  so new plants can be licensed again — while still rejecting the initiative itself as too
  rigid. Both chambers of parliament had, as of late 2025, voted in favor of the
  counter-proposal.
- **Status:** unresolved at time of writing (August 2026) — a public referendum on both the
  initiative and the counter-proposal is expected. Opposition comes from the Greens, Social
  Democrats, and Green Liberals.
- **Why it matters for the case study:** even a "yes" outcome doesn't add supply inside the
  10-year window this case study covers — see [[Swiss Nuclear Decommissioning Timeline]].
