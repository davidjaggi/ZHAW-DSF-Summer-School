# Imports and Grid Interconnection

A necessary backstop, not a domestic investment option — and the thing the
[[Mantelerlass 2024]] explicitly caps.

- **Role:** Switzerland sits at the heart of the continental European grid (bordering 5
  countries), both depending on and providing balancing services to neighbors — its energy
  security is inseparable from European market and grid conditions.
- **The winter pattern:** Switzerland is a net electricity **exporter in summer** and a net
  **importer in winter** — see [[Winter Import Gap]] for the mechanics and the legal cap.
- **Cost:** not free — imports are priced at the marginal European price, which was extremely
  volatile in 2022–2023 and remains structurally higher in winter than Swiss domestic hydro
  cost. Every TWh of import avoided by domestic investment has hedging value beyond its own
  LCOE.
- **Policy:** [[Mantelerlass 2024]] caps net winter electricity imports at 5 TWh/year,
  explicitly targeting this gap as binding federal policy.
- **Energy security tension:** a genuine reliability asset (European market access,
  historically a transit/balancing hub) increasingly in tension with the political goal of
  reducing import dependence.
