# Nuclear Power

Switzerland's aging but still-critical winter baseload, with its legal future genuinely
undecided.

- **Current role:** ~24–29% of generation. 4 reactors after Mühleberg's 2019 shutdown:
  Beznau I & II, Gösgen, Leibstadt. See [[Swiss Nuclear Decommissioning Timeline]].
- **Cost:** the existing fleet is a sunk asset with low marginal cost — some of the cheapest
  electricity Switzerland has. A *new* plant would be the most expensive, highest-risk option
  by far (international comparators point to $0.10–0.19/kWh, with large cost/schedule
  overruns at Flamanville and Hinkley Point C).
- **Reliability:** high capacity factor, baseload, weather-independent — historically the
  most reliable winter source. The existing fleet is aging (Beznau I is one of the world's
  oldest operating reactors), so safety-driven maintenance already causes year-to-year output
  swings.
- **Land use:** the smallest footprint of any option (cited as ~18–34x more land-efficient
  than hydro/solar per unit energy), but siting is highly constrained by cooling water,
  seismic, and population-density rules.
- **Public acceptance:** genuinely unresolved — the 2017 referendum banned new plants; the
  [[Swiss Nuclear Ban Referendum]] (2024–2026) has reopened the question, with the outcome
  still pending.
- **Environmental impact:** very low lifecycle carbon emissions. The real issues are
  long-lived radioactive waste (a deep geological repository is decades from operation) and
  low-probability, high-consequence accident risk.
- **Scalability:** effectively zero within a 10-year window, even if the ban is lifted.
- **Implementation time:** 15–20+ years realistically for a new plant — see
  [[Swiss Nuclear Decommissioning Timeline]].
