# Wind Power

Small in scale today, cheap in principle, but the most locally contested renewable
technology in Switzerland.

- **Current role:** <1% of generation; only ~50 turbines nationwide, far below potential and
  behind its target trajectory.
- **Cost:** cheapest LCOE globally (~$0.034/kWh), but Alpine/Jura terrain (transport,
  foundations, grid connection) raises Swiss costs above the flat-terrain average. The total
  cost of reaching the modest 4.3 TWh/year long-term target is manageable in absolute terms.
- **Reliability:** complementary to solar (relatively stronger in winter, especially with
  Bise/föhn wind patterns), but current scale is too small to move the reliability needle
  much on its own.
- **Land use:** low *direct* footprint, high *visual/spatial* footprint — turbine pads are
  small, but each turbine needs wide spacing and access roads; land underneath often stays
  usable.
- **Public acceptance:** the most locally contested renewable technology in Switzerland
  today. Two dedicated federal popular initiatives were validated in September 2025
  specifically targeting wind — see [[Policy and Legal Context]].
- **Environmental impact:** very low operational carbon; localized concerns are bird/bat
  strikes, noise, and forest clearance for turbine pads/access roads — the explicit subject
  of one of the 2025 anti-wind initiatives.
- **Scalability:** low-to-moderate in absolute terms, but large in relative/percentage terms
  off a tiny base.
- **Implementation time:** 5–10+ years typical, longer where contested.
