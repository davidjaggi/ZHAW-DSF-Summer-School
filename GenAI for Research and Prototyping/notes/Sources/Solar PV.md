# Solar PV

The single largest realistic growth lever for the next decade — and the statutory target
Switzerland is legally committed to hitting.

- **Current role:** ~10–13% and rising fast; installed capacity roughly tripled 2020–2025 —
  the fastest-growing source.
- **Cost:** the cheapest new-build source available (global benchmark ~$0.043/kWh); Plateau
  solar is cost-competitive, while alpine solar ([[Alpine Solar (Solarexpress)]]) costs more
  per kWh but delivers disproportionate winter output.
- **Reliability / energy security:** structurally weak in winter at low altitude (lowland PV
  produces roughly 3–4x more in summer than winter) — the weakest contributor to security
  despite being cheapest and most scalable. Alpine PV is a partial fix, generating up to ~half
  its annual output in winter.
- **Land use:** medium–high — ground-mount panels fully occupy the land beneath them,
  competing with agriculture on the Plateau or with alpine/pastoral land at altitude.
- **Public acceptance:** alpine solar is mixed but workable — roughly half of
  Solarexpress-type projects have been approved where put to a local vote.
- **Environmental impact:** very low operational impact; the lifecycle footprint is dominated
  by manufacturing (mostly abroad) and eventual panel recycling.
- **Scalability:** by far the largest realistic growth lever for the next decade.
- **Implementation time:** months for rooftop/small solar; 2–4 years for ground-mount/alpine
  projects under the Solarexpress fast-track.
- **Statutory driver:** [[Mantelerlass 2024]] sets the binding 35 TWh/year-by-2035 new
  renewables target that solar must carry most of.
