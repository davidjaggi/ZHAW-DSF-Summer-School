# Research Note 1 — Switzerland's Current Electricity Mix

## Snapshot (2024/2025)

| Source | Share of generation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hydropower (large + small, incl. pumped storage) | ~55–60% | Backbone of the system; storage hydro is Switzerland's main dispatchable and flexible asset. |
| Nuclear | ~24–29% | 4 reactors in operation (Beznau I & II, Gösgen, Leibstadt) after Mühleberg's 2019 shutdown; no new plants may currently be built (law under revision, see Note 2). |
| Solar PV | ~10–13% and rising fast | Fastest-growing source; installed capacity roughly tripled 2020–2025. |
| Wind | <1% | Only ~50 turbines nationwide; far below potential and behind target trajectory. |
| Other renewables (biomass, biogas, small hydro already counted above, waste-to-energy) | ~5% | Biomass potential only ~50% exploited. |
| Fossil (gas/oil, mostly backup/district heat CHP) | <5% | Marginal role in electricity; larger role in heating and transport (outside this report's scope, which is electricity). |

Switzerland's electricity supply is already >90% low-carbon (hydro + nuclear + new renewables combined), which is unusual internationally and shapes the debate: the question is not "coal vs. clean" but **which clean mix** replaces retiring nuclear and closes the winter gap.

## Structural features that matter for the case study

- **Seasonal mismatch.** Hydro reservoirs and nuclear produce relatively evenly across the year, but solar is heavily skewed to summer. Switzerland is a **net electricity exporter in summer and a net importer in winter** — importing on average ~4.5 TWh/winter over the last decade (peak 9.7 TWh in 2016/17), about 15% of winter demand. This "winter gap" is the central planning problem, not aggregate annual supply.
- **No coal, limited gas.** Unlike most of Europe, fossil generation is structurally marginal, so the marginal investment decision is really "hydro flexibility vs. nuclear extension/new-build vs. solar/wind buildout vs. imports."
- **Demand growth.** Electrification of heating (heat pumps) and transport (EVs), plus data centers/AI compute, is expected to raise electricity demand even as total final energy consumption falls (efficiency gains elsewhere). The Energy Strategy 2050 targets ~53% lower **per-capita** energy consumption vs. 2000, but electricity's *share* of final energy is rising.
- **Grid interconnection.** Switzerland sits at the heart of the continental European grid (5 countries border it) and both depends on and provides balancing services to neighbors — its energy security is inseparable from European market and grid conditions.

## Forward look

Renewable power generation (excl. large hydro) is forecast to grow from ~11.4 TWh (2024) to ~31.4 TWh by 2035 (~9.7% CAGR), driven mainly by solar. The Mantelerlass (Umbrella Act, see Note 2) sets a formal target of **35 TWh/year from new renewables by 2035** (vs. ~6 TWh in 2022) and an *indicative* 45 TWh by 2050, on top of existing hydro (~38 TWh/year average) and any remaining nuclear.
