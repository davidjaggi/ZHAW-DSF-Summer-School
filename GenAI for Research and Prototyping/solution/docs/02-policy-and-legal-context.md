# Research Note 2 — Policy & Legal Context

## Energy Strategy 2050 (the founding framework)

Adopted after the 2011 Fukushima accident, Switzerland's Energy Strategy 2050 set the direction still in force today: phase out nuclear power over time (no lifetime limit, but **no new nuclear plants may be built** under the 2017 Nuclear Energy Act), cut per-capita energy consumption, and expand renewables. It targets raising renewable electricity production from 46.5 TWh to 83.8 TWh (a near doubling, hydro included) and a 53% cut in per-capita energy use vs. 2000.

## The Climate and Innovation Act (2023)

Enshrines a **net-zero greenhouse gas target for 2050**, with interim milestones of 75% reduction by 2040 and 89% by 2050 (vs. 1990). This is the backdrop against which every electricity-mix decision must now be judged — the question is not just security of supply but decarbonization.

## The "Mantelerlass" / Federal Act on a Secure Electricity Supply with Renewable Energies (2024)

The single most important recent legal change. Approved by **68.7% of voters on 9 June 2024** and in force since **1 January 2025**, it:

- Sets a binding target of **35 TWh/year of new renewable electricity (solar, wind, biomass, small hydro) by 2035**, versus ~6 TWh/year in 2022 — roughly a six-fold increase.
- Caps **maximum net winter electricity imports at 5 TWh**, explicitly targeting the seasonal security-of-supply gap.
- Streamlines permitting: designates renewable projects "of national interest," and (per the 2025/2026 follow-up "acceleration law," in force from **1 April 2026**) requires cantons to set up a **single consolidated approval process** for large solar and wind projects to cut multi-year permitting delays.
- Introduces new funding/support instruments for production, grid, storage, and demand flexibility.
- Extended the **Solarexpress** fast-track for large ground-mounted/alpine PV plants through the transition period.

## Nuclear: the ban is being actively contested (2025–2026, still unresolved)

- The **"Stop the Blackout" popular initiative** (launched March 2024 by business/conservative groups) seeks a constitutional guarantee of secure, climate-neutral electricity supply, implicitly reopening the door to nuclear.
- In 2025 the Federal Council proposed an **indirect counter-proposal**: amend the Nuclear Energy Act so new nuclear plants can be licensed again, reversing the 2017 policy — while still rejecting the initiative itself as too rigid.
- Both chambers of parliament (Senate and National Council) had, as of late 2025, voted in favor of the counter-proposal.
- **A public referendum on both the initiative and the counter-proposal is expected** — outcome not yet known at the time of writing (Aug 2026). Opposition comes from the Greens, Social Democrats, and Green Liberals, who argue it contradicts the 2017 popular vote to phase out nuclear.

**Implication for this case study:** any recommendation must be robust to *both* outcomes — a mix that still works whether or not new nuclear becomes legally possible again, since the legal status is genuinely undecided.

## Wind-specific legal friction

Two federal popular initiatives validated in September 2025 — "Against the destruction of our forests by wind turbines" and "For the protection of direct democracy regarding wind parks" — specifically target wind projects, reflecting the strongest source of local political friction of any technology in the mix (see Note 5).

## Implication for policy design

Swiss energy policy is made through **direct democracy at three levels** (federal, cantonal, communal), so legal "targets" are necessary but not sufficient — actual buildout depends on winning local votes project-by-project. Policy recommendations must include acceptance-building instruments (revenue sharing, participation models), not just top-down targets.
